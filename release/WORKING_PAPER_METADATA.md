# Working-paper metadata — draft only

**Title:** Persistent Records and Rehabilitation Incentives

**Author:** Aayush Kadam

**Version:** v0.1.0 — first public external-review working paper

**Date:** September 2026

**Abstract:** Past behavior can predict future behavior and discipline current actions. When agents can invest in changing their future productive state, however, a persistent public record also changes the return to that investment. This paper studies a finite-horizon dynamic control problem that separates persistence of an institutional score from persistence of underlying quality. On a nonempty region, greater score persistence strengthens ex ante discipline while reducing productive effort after an adverse history. Records can also produce differences in future quality rather than merely reveal them: even with zero intrinsic quality persistence, different records generate different next-period quality whenever they induce different effort. Repeated score-dependent effort can propagate these differences over a finite horizon. The paper establishes neither a universal case for forgetting nor a universal optimal record duration.

**Keywords:** reputation; institutional records; dynamic incentives; rehabilitation; endogenous quality

**JEL codes:** D82 (asymmetric and private information; mechanism design); D83 (information, learning, communication, and belief); D86 (contract theory)

**Short description:** A finite-horizon theory note separating institutional record persistence from intrinsic quality persistence and identifying a regional tension between deterrence and rehabilitation.
