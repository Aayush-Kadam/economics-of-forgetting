# Persistent Records and Rehabilitation Incentives

**Author:** Aayush Kadam

## Research question

How does the persistence of a public institutional record jointly affect discipline before an adverse event and productive rehabilitation afterward?

## Model in one paragraph

The paper studies a finite-horizon dynamic control problem in which an institutional score summarizes public history and determines opportunity through a reduced-form schedule. A clean agent may take a privately beneficial harmful action; after an adverse event, the agent can invest in productive effort that raises future quality. The score and true quality have distinct persistence parameters. This separation makes it possible to identify effects produced by the institution’s record rather than by mechanical persistence of fundamentals.

## Core contribution 1

There exists a nonempty open region in which greater score persistence strengthens ex ante discipline while reducing productive post-event effort. The result is regional, not a global monotonicity claim.

## Core contribution 2

When intrinsic quality persistence is zero, different records can still create different next-period quality through record-dependent effort. Repeated effort differences can propagate the gap over a finite horizon.

## Closest literature

Board and Meyer-ter-Vehn (2013), Bohren (2024), Bhaskar and Thomas (2019), Elul and Gottardi (2015), Liu and Skrzypacz (2014), and Pei (forthcoming).

## What is different

The same institutional persistence parameter governs a pre-event discipline margin and a post-event productive-investment margin, while record persistence is explicitly separated from intrinsic state persistence.

## Main limitation

The opportunity schedule is reduced form, the model is finite horizon, and the central comparative static is regional. The paper does not establish a universal case for forgetting or an optimal record half-life.

## Specific questions for the reader

Is the joint lifecycle theorem sufficiently distinct and interesting? Is the zero-intrinsic-persistence result economically informative rather than mechanical? Which assumption most limits publishability, and what single revision would improve the paper most?
