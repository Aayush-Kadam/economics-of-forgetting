# M1.5 Hostile Internal Audit

## Scope

This audit independently re-derives M1 and searches for conditions under which its headline mechanism fails. The baseline remains a mechanical institutional score, not a Bayesian belief.

## 1. Recovery mechanics

Let `R_{t+1}=phi R_t+(1-phi)y_G`, with `R_0<R_bar<y_G`. Iteration gives

`R_t=y_G+phi^t(R_0-y_G)`.

The continuous crossing time is `n=log q/log phi`, where `q=(y_G-R_bar)/(y_G-R_0)` lies in `(0,1)`. Direct differentiation gives

`n_phi=-log(q)/[phi(log phi)^2]>0`.

This is global on the stated domain. The integer crossing time is a weakly increasing step function. At `phi=0`, crossing takes one discrete update; at `phi -> 1`, it diverges. If `y_G<=R_bar`, there is no finite crossing. The M1 result is correct.

## 2. Binary rehabilitation

Rehabilitation is chosen when `beta^{n(phi)}V>=C`. With `0<beta<1`, the return is strictly decreasing in `phi`; an interior threshold exists only when `C/V` lies strictly between the endpoint returns. At `beta=1`, delay alone is irrelevant. If access produces flow benefits foregone during recovery, discouragement can remain even for a patient agent; that is a different payoff specification. M1's theorem is correct but not universal.

## 3. Continuous effort: fresh derivation

Let `m=phi^T`, `x_1=rho x+alpha e`, and

`r=mR+(1-m)x_1`.

The objective is `beta^T V A(r)-kappa e^2/2`. An interior optimum satisfies

`F(e,phi)=beta^T V alpha(1-m)A'(r)-kappa e=0`.

For a strict local maximum,

`D = kappa-beta^T V[alpha(1-m)]^2 A''(r)>0`.

Implicit differentiation yields

`e_phi = beta^T V alpha m'[-A'(r)+(1-m)A''(r)(R-x_1)]/D`,

where `m'=T phi^{T-1}`. Two forces are distinct:

1. **Attenuation:** `-A'(r)`. More persistent records place less weight on quality created by current effort. This always lowers the marginal return when `A'>0`.
2. **Relocation:** `(1-m)A''(r)(R-x_1)`. More persistence relocates the future score along a curved opportunity schedule; this may reinforce or offset attenuation.

Thus the logistic sign is primitive-driven only after conditioning on state and curvature; it is not globally negative.

## 4. Logistic opportunity and the sign map

For `A(r)=1/[1+exp(-k(r-R_bar))]`,

`A''(r)=k A'(r)[1-2A(r)]`.

Hence, at an interior strict optimum,

`sign(e_phi)=sign{-1+(1-m)k[1-2A(r)](R-x_1)}`.

The denominator is positive by the second-order condition. The sign depends on distance from the logistic midpoint, the record-quality gap, slope `k`, and horizon through `m`. `beta,V,alpha,kappa,rho` affect the equilibrium location and whether the solution is interior, even where they cancel from the normalized sign expression.

The generated state map shows negative, positive, boundary, and nonconcave regions. Global monotonicity is false.

## 5. Effort as a function of reputation

At a strict interior optimum,

`e_R = beta^T V alpha(1-m)m A''(r)/D`.

For the logistic map, effort rises with initial reputation when the induced future score lies below the midpoint, peaks around the high-slope recovery region, and falls after the midpoint. This supports three mathematical regions, with qualifications:

- **Low-return region:** very low scores have small `A'` and low effort.
- **Recovery region:** scores near the logistic midpoint have high `A'` and high effort.
- **Safe region:** high scores have declining marginal reputational returns.

The labels describe marginal incentives, not multiple equilibria or a theorem of hopelessness.

## 6. Why linear opportunity remains useful

When `A(r)=a+br`, `A''=0`. The relocation force vanishes and

`e*=beta^T V b alpha(1-phi^T)/kappa`,

subject to bounds. This cleanly proves attenuation but makes effort independent of initial reputation. Curvature—or state dependence generated by a continuation value—is necessary for record histories to change marginal effort. The coherent architecture is therefore: linear opportunity for the global persistence theorem; smooth nonlinear opportunity for state-dependent recovery incentives.

## 7. Mechanical versus behavioral decomposition

For any fixed post-event signal path, changing `phi` changes `R_t` mechanically. Allowing effort to respond changes the signal through `x_1=rho x+alpha e*(phi)`. The total score difference can be written as:

`R_t(phi,e*(phi))-R_t(phi_0,e*(phi_0))`

`=[R_t(phi,e_0)-R_t(phi_0,e_0)] + [R_t(phi,e*(phi))-R_t(phi,e_0)]`.

The first bracket is the mechanical score effect holding behavior fixed. The second is the behavioral component. Code-generated paths verify that these are distinct. This is not yet a statistical prediction decomposition.

## 8. Misconduct audit

In the M1 fresh-start model, `P(phi)=V[1-beta^{n(phi)}]` increases globally and aggregate misconduct weakly falls, strictly so away from probability bounds. This conclusion requires that detection moves the agent from valuable immediate access into the recovery path.

It is not globally valid across lifecycle states. If an already-stigmatized agent has no remaining access to lose, if punishment is capped, if the horizon ends before recovery, or if a persistent bad record reduces the opportunity cost of further misconduct, the derivative may be zero and can become positive under added substitution effects. M2 therefore states deterrence for clean agents with an unsaturated continuation-value loss, not for every reputation state.

## 9. Lifecycle-asymmetry survival

- Binary rehabilitation: survives for an interior cost range.
- Continuous linear opportunity: survives globally while the solution is interior.
- Smooth logistic opportunity: survives on a nonempty state region characterized by the IFT inequality above.
- Finite horizon: survives.
- Infinite horizon: not established by M1 and must not be inferred.
- Noisy signals: expected-score linearity preserves Model 0 under mean-zero noise, but nonlinear opportunity introduces Jensen effects; no general theorem yet.
- Different initial reputations: deterrence and rehabilitation effects are state-dependent and can saturate.

The correct claim is regional: a nonempty set of clean/adverse lifecycle states supports opposite incentive effects from the same `phi`.

## 10. Strengthened placebos

1. `alpha=0`: effort cannot alter quality; the productive channel vanishes.
2. Constant opportunity: reputation-induced effort vanishes.
3. `rho=0`: reputation can still generate a next-period quality gap through `alpha e`; repeated effort is required for persistence beyond that in the stripped model.
4. `phi=0`: the next signal fully replaces the record; rehabilitation has maximum score responsiveness and delay deterrence is weakest.
5. `phi->1`: score responsiveness vanishes. Linear effort tends to zero; logistic effort also tends to zero except at boundaries/nonunique limiting choices. Binary recovery time diverges.
6. Perfect observation of `x`: public-history effects vanish, although ordinary productive investment can remain.
7. Zero signal noise: the mechanism survives; noise is not essential.
8. `beta->1`: the fixed-horizon attenuation effect survives through score weights, but pure-delay binary discouragement disappears absent foregone flow.
9. `kappa->0`: effort reaches its upper bound where any marginal return exists, making local memory effects saturate.
10. Very large `alpha`: effort may fall because little effort is needed or hit a boundary; high productivity can overcome inertia but does not restore global monotonicity.

## 11. Normalized economics

The linear interior effort depends on the composite incentive strength

`I = beta^T V b alpha/kappa`

times score responsiveness `1-phi^T`. Logistic local signs are governed by the dimensionless curvature-record-gap term

`C = (1-phi^T)[A''(r)/A'(r)](R-x_1)`.

Memory reduces effort when `C<1`, conditional on the second-order condition. For logistic opportunity, `A''/A'=k(1-2A)`.

## Audit changes to M1

T1–T4 survive under their conditions. T5 must be reframed as a finite-horizon regional coexistence result. T6 is analytically conditionally true because `e_R` has the sign of `A''` at strict interior optima; it is not merely numerical. Infinite-horizon, noisy-signal, and predictiveness claims remain open.
