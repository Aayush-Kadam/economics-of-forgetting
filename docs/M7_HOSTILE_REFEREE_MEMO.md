# M7 hostile referee memo

## Recommendation

Major revision; do not circulate as a broad optimal-forgetting paper.

## Central objection

The surviving “two-state memory” rule is implemented by a clean/adverse lifecycle flag. That makes the computation at least as naturally a punishment-state result as a memory-design result. The paper must prove or quantify what score dynamics add beyond a temporary punishment flag.

## Evidence in its favor

The sign survives 61 implemented re-optimizations, two limited heterogeneity mixtures, recurrence stresses, T=3–50, and a bounded continuous effort check. It is not a single-grid accident.

## Evidence against a strong claim

Magnitude nearly vanishes under linear access and some populations; M6 LARGE labels shrink on refinement; alternative timing, stochastic false positives, rolling windows, and strategic gaming remain unsolved. Costless direct punishment beats the baseline no-penalty memory design, although memory adds value when combined with that penalty.

## Required revision

Reframe around a constrained lifecycle-contingent information/punishment rule, make direct-instrument constraints explicit, and treat memory as one implementation. A theory-first paper is more credible than a universal computational design claim.
