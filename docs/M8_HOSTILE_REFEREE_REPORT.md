# M8 hostile referee report

## Recommendation

**MAJOR REVISION.** The draft is a coherent theory note, but it is not yet competitive as a broad reputation-design paper.

## 1. What is genuinely new?

The plausible increment is narrow: one institutional score-persistence parameter has opposed pre-event misconduct and post-event productive-effort effects on a nonempty region. The ingredients are established separately. The paper should claim a clean synthesis and regional theorem, not a new literature on forgetting.

## 2. Is lifecycle asymmetry obvious dynamic punishment?

Partly. The deterrence side is standard. The nontrivial step is coupling the same persistence parameter to an investment that changes future quality and showing the opposing effects coexist on an open set. The contribution depends on keeping this joint statement precise.

## 3. Is institution-produced persistence distinct?

Only modestly. Board-Meyer-ter-Vehn and Bohren already connect reputation, investment, and persistent quality. The zero-rho identity cleanly separates score memory from state memory, but it is a supporting decomposition rather than a standalone breakthrough.

## 4. Is the score-based setup too reduced-form?

For a short theory note, no; for a general reputation paper, yes. The manuscript correctly calls the opportunity schedule reduced form. It must keep equilibrium language out and identify Bayesian market response as future work.

## 5. Does finite horizon weaken the contribution?

Yes. Terminal incentives and the absence of a stationary equilibrium limit generality. The analytical one-investment results remain valid, but dynamic extrapolation must stay finite-horizon.

## 6. Is the zero-rho result substantive?

It is clarifying rather than deep. Its value is diagnostic: any next-period gap at rho=0 is behaviorally produced. Claims of permanent or stationary persistence would be unjustified.

## 7. Are the feedback regions general enough?

They are local and conditional on interiority. The A'' sign characterization is useful, but bounds and nonconcavity prevent a global partition. Present it as mechanism discipline, not a third contribution.

## 8. Is the policy section worth retaining?

Not in the main text. The two-state result is model-specific, close to a punishment flag, and sometimes negligible. Move it entirely to the appendix and summarize it in one introduction sentence.

## 9. Does direct punishment undermine design?

Yes. A strong costless penalty beats baseline memory alone. The complementarity result is interesting but instrument-grid specific. It belongs among limitations.

## 10. Is quantitative magnitude compelling?

No. The benchmark gain is 0.64 percent of the feasible range. Larger atlas values shrink under refinement. The computation demonstrates possibility and robustness of sign, not policy importance.

## 11. Is the literature adequate?

The closest papers are acknowledged directly. Hamilton-Cui and Gjerde-Slotnick remain access risks and should be disclosed in repository documentation, not papered over with a priority claim.

## 12. Are any claims too strong?

The abstract's design sentence is acceptable but unnecessary. The main-text design section overweights a secondary result. “Produce future fundamentals” should always be tied to productive effort and model conditions.

## 13. What would cause rejection?

Pitching the paper as a universal optimal-forgetting result; presenting a control problem as equilibrium; claiming novelty for reputation-dependent investment; or retaining the numerical policy exercise as coequal with the theory.

## 14. Highest-value revision

Turn the draft into a narrow theory note: two core propositions, regional mechanism qualification, design material in the appendix, and explicit limits. A Bayesian or infinite-horizon extension would raise ambition but is a new research program and should not be improvised in M8.
