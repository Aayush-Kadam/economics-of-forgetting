# Novelty and Collision Audit

**Search date:** 2026-09-19  
**Gate verdict:** **M0.5 CLOSED — PROCEED TO M1 WITH FURTHER NARROWING**

## Executive verdict

The original broad framing is not novel. Economics already contains fading and bounded reputation memory, explicit reputation half-lives, reputation-dependent quality investment, welfare analysis of forgetting, expungement with deterrence, and information concealment that changes human-capital investment. Those components cannot be branded as new.

The search did not identify a single verified paper combining all of the following as its central object:

1. an institutionally chosen continuous memory persistence or half-life;
2. pre-adverse-event misconduct and deterrence;
3. post-adverse-event costly rehabilitation that changes a persistent underlying productive/behavioral state;
4. reputation-dependent opportunities that alter the return to rehabilitation; and
5. a structural decomposition of old records’ apparent predictiveness into informational persistence and persistence produced by the record rule itself.

That joint object is a plausible research gap. It is narrow, collision-prone, and not yet a demonstrated contribution. M1 is authorized only after a theorem-by-theorem full-text check of the two recent working-paper risks identified below.

## Search protocol

### Wave 1 — canonical reputation and dynamic quality

Queries combined “reputation,” “fading/limited/bounded memory,” “dynamic moral hazard,” “endogenous quality,” “quality investment,” “half-life,” and “persistent actions.” This identified Board and Meyer-ter-Vehn (2013, 2022), Bohren (2024), Liu and Skrzypacz (2014), Sperisen (2018), Gjerde and Slotnick (2004), and Hauser (2024).

### Wave 2 — record retention, credit, and legal forgetting

Queries combined “credit reporting,” “record retention,” “bankruptcy,” “forgetting,” “right to be forgotten,” “expungement,” “record sealing,” “repayment incentives,” and “second chances.” This identified Elul and Gottardi (2015), Brown and Zehnder (2007), Mungan (2017), and Kim and Kim (2017).

### Wave 3 — labor-market concealment and substitution to group priors

Queries combined “Ban the Box,” “human-capital investment,” “statistical discrimination,” “criminal records,” “rehabilitation,” and “group priors.” This identified Patty and Penn (2022), the empirical Ban-the-Box literature, and evidence on criminal-record remediation.

### Wave 4 — recent rating design

Queries combined “fresh rating systems,” “stale ratings,” “rating freshness,” “reputation decay,” “optimal memory,” and “platform incentives.” This identified Hamilton and Cui (2024), which is a mandatory full-text collision check.

Publisher pages, DOI records, AEA pages, accessible full text, SSRN/arXiv records, and institutional repositories were used to verify existence and bibliographic identity. Google Scholar was not directly accessible in this environment; citation-chain coverage is therefore incomplete and explicitly treated as a residual risk.

## Closest works

### 1. Bohren (2024): closest mechanism-level theory

Bohren studies effort that changes a persistent payoff-relevant state, including product quality and ratings as interpretations, in a dynamic moral-hazard game. This destroys any claim that linking persistent effort, quality, and reputation-dependent opportunity is new. The proposed project differs only if memory of records is a separately chosen institutional state and if the same policy has opposing effects before and after a bad event.

### 2. Gjerde and Slotnick (2004): closest terminology and state-control collision

This paper explicitly uses a reputation “half-life” and models short- and long-term quality efforts. The project may use the term descriptively, but it cannot present “reputation half-life affects quality effort” as a new insight. The model must instead derive a strategic recovery wedge after an adverse history and an institutional welfare choice.

### 3. Elul and Gottardi (2015): closest regulated-forgetting welfare collision

They analyze limits on access to past defaults in repeated credit markets with moral hazard and adverse selection and show conditions under which forgetting is welfare improving. A generic theorem that “forgetting can improve welfare” is therefore non-novel.

### 4. Sperisen (2018): closest fading-memory mechanism design collision

Fading memory can outperform bounded memory by limiting reputational echoes while retaining information. “An intermediate fading-memory design can be beneficial” is not a novel contribution.

### 5. Hamilton and Cui (2024): highest unresolved recent-paper risk

The available record describes a platform design paper addressing stale reviews, incentives, penalties, and fees. Its full theorem set must be inspected before asserting a rating-system design contribution.

### 6. Mungan (2017) and Patty–Penn (2022): policy-channel collisions

Mungan connects expungement to deterrence, while Patty and Penn connect concealment of applicant information to human-capital investment and statistical discrimination. These eliminate broad claims that second chances, deterrence, investment, and group-prior substitution have not been studied.

## Answers to the ten gate questions

### 1. What is already known?

Limited or fading records alter reputational equilibria; reputation changes costly real investment; persistent effort can sustain dynamic incentives; regulated forgetting can improve or reduce welfare; deletion/concealment can weaken discipline or shift inference to group characteristics; and stale-rating designs can change platform incentives.

### 2. What is closest to the proposed mechanism?

Bohren (persistent effort/quality and ratings), Gjerde–Slotnick (reputation half-life and quality effort), Board–Meyer-ter-Vehn and Hauser (low-reputation investment and recovery), Elul–Gottardi (regulated forgetting with incentives and welfare), and Mungan (expungement and deterrence).

### 3. What exact claims would be non-novel?

- Reputation affects real quality investment.
- Limited/fading memory changes behavior.
- A finite or intermediate memory rule can dominate permanent memory in some environments.
- Deleting records can improve access but weaken incentives.
- Concealing individual history can induce statistical discrimination.
- Expungement may affect deterrence and later behavior.
- Reputation can be path dependent or cyclical.

### 4. What exact contribution may survive?

A formal separation between the **pre-record discipline elasticity** and the **post-record rehabilitation elasticity** with respect to the same memory parameter, when rehabilitation changes a persistent underlying state; conditions under which the elasticities have opposite signs; and a decomposition showing how the institution’s memory rule changes the future outcome process whose predictability is being evaluated.

### 5. Which terminology already exists?

“Fading memory,” “bounded memory,” “limited records,” “impermanent reputations,” “reputation half-life,” “fresh rating systems,” “reputation cycles,” “expungement,” and “right to be forgotten” all pre-exist. “Endogenous predictiveness” may be usable only after a broader terminology search and a formal definition.

## M5 targeted collision finding

Forgiveness after punishment, readmission after ostracism, forgiving repeated-game strategies, reputation repair, and manipulation-resistant reputation mechanisms all pre-exist. In particular, Ali and Miller (2016) show that forgiveness can improve ostracism. Therefore **earned forgetting**, **forgiveness improves incentives**, and **conditional readmission** are prohibited as broad novelty claims. The remaining candidate contribution is the narrower joint comparison of lifecycle-specific score persistence, productive rehabilitation, endogenous future quality, welfare, and prediction. This remains collision-sensitive and is not publication-ready.

### 6. Which proposed theorems risk rediscovery?

Rehabilitation collapse overlaps low-reputation investment cutoffs; hysteresis overlaps path-dependent reputation dynamics; interior memory overlaps fading/limited-record design; earned forgetting overlaps conditional expungement and endogenous disclosure; statistical-discrimination substitution overlaps Ban-the-Box theory and evidence.

### 7. What should be dropped?

Drop any headline claim that finite memory, reputation decay, rehabilitation incentives, or a welfare tradeoff is itself new. Drop “reputation half-life” as a claimed coined term. Do not lead with a generic right-to-be-forgotten policy argument.

### 8. What should be reframed?

Frame the paper around an **asymmetry across lifecycle states**: the same memory technology can discipline an unblemished agent yet depress productive investment by an agent already carrying an adverse record. Treat informational accuracy and behavioral production of future quality as distinct welfare channels.

### 9. What is the narrowest defensible contribution?

In a transparent dynamic model, derive conditions under which increasing record persistence raises the penalty from initial misconduct but lowers optimal recovery investment after a bad record, and show that the latter channel disappears when rehabilitation productivity or reputation-dependent opportunity is shut down.

### 10. What is the strongest defensible contribution?

If later proved and robust to smooth market responses: characterize the optimal memory rule when records both reveal persistent quality and change that quality through investment incentives, identify when apparent long-horizon predictiveness is partly institution-produced, and show whether performance-contingent forgetting improves on a constant half-life without being easily gamed.

## Collision decision

No **fatal** single-paper collision was located, but several components face **high** collision risk. M0 therefore passes only with material narrowing. M1 must begin with the minimal distinction between a record state and an underlying productive state; otherwise the model collapses into existing dynamic reputation/quality work.

## M7 targeted re-check (2026-09-19)

The closest conceptual collision is bounded-memory community enforcement, which already studies temporary exclusion after transgression and shows that coarse records can sustain punishment. Endogenous-record reputation work also studies strategic erasure. Product-recall work explicitly compares reputation with a tax/subsidy intervention. The project therefore cannot claim novelty for temporary punishment, bounded records, or direct-instrument comparisons as such. The potentially distinct object is narrower: optimal constant versus lifecycle-contingent exponential persistence when rehabilitation effort and score-induced opportunity are jointly endogenous.

## M7.5 final freeze

The final crosswalk caps novelty at MODERATE. Board and Meyer-ter-Vehn already establish reputation-dependent investment in endogenous quality; Bohren establishes dynamic incentives from persistent payoff-relevant quality; Bhaskar and Thomas establish bounded-memory punishment states; Pei studies strategic record erasure; and Jovanovic compares reputation with direct instruments. The manuscript may claim only the regional combination of pre-event discipline and post-event productive rehabilitation under one institutional score-persistence parameter, plus the transparent zero-rho decomposition as a supporting result. Regional feedback is not an independent novelty claim.
